The Art of Pandan Weaving

Pandan weaving is a traditional Filipino craft passed down through generations. In the town of Luisiana, Laguna—known as the “Home of Pandan Products”—artisans skillfully transform dried Pandanus simplex leaves into handwoven creations such as bags, mats, hats, and baskets. This craft reflects both artistry and resourcefulness, turning nature’s abundance into beautiful, functional works of art.

Every year on April 3, Luisiana celebrates the Pandanan Festival, honoring pandan products that have long supported the town’s economy. For many locals, pandan weaving is more than a livelihood—it’s a cultural tradition that strengthens community ties and preserves Filipino craftsmanship.

Weaving pandan leaves showcases creativity, patience, and environmental care. By using natural materials found in their surroundings, artisans promote sustainable living while keeping traditional skills alive for the next generation. (Eco-friendly Handicrafts: The Art of Filipino Pandan Leaves Weaving, n.d.)

Did you know?

According to Ebs (2025), pandan — also known as screwpine — is a tropical plant loved across Southeast Asia for its sweet aroma and vibrant green color. Its leaves flavor rice, desserts, and drinks, while also offering health benefits like aiding digestion and soothing the body. Beyond cooking, pandan leaves are used in traditional crafts and home remedies — proving that this humble plant is as useful as it is fragrant!

How Haplos Breeze is Made

1

Cutting the Leaves

Pandan leaves are split into four strips, prepared for weaving.

2

Starting the Weave

Strips are woven in an alternating over-and-under pattern to form the square base.

3

Building the Pattern

More strips are added and glued for a firm structure.

4

Shaping the Fan

The base is trimmed, layered, and backed with abaca for strength.

5

Attaching the Handle

A bamboo stick wrapped in pandan weave is added for grip.

6

Finishing Touches

Decorated with ribbons, flowers, and details for its final Hablos Breeze touch.
